01 / Theoretical Foundations

Key Concepts

Pedagogical Communication

Professional interaction between teachers and students that supports learning, dialogue and interpersonal development.

  • Exchange of knowledge
  • Active listening
  • Constructive feedback
  • Mutual understanding

Acmeology

The interdisciplinary study of personal and professional development, with particular attention to achieving high levels of competence and mastery.

Central principle: continuous professional growth.

02 / Educational Significance

The Functions of Communication

01 — Educational

Supports the acquisition and understanding of knowledge.

02 — Motivational

Encourages engagement, curiosity and active participation.

03 — Developmental

Strengthens critical thinking, creativity and independence.

04 — Relational

Establishes trust, cooperation and mutual respect.

03 / Humanistic Educational Thought

Haim Ginott: The Ethics of Communication

Haim Ginott (1922–1973)

Psychologist and educator.

Teacher and Child (1972)

  • Respect the student's dignity.
  • Avoid personal humiliation.
  • Address behavior, not identity.
  • Maintain a supportive classroom climate.

Communication Reframing

“You are a bad student.”

Preserve dignity while addressing behavior.

04 / Comparative Analysis

Pedagogical Communication Styles

Authoritarian

Teacher-centered decisions, strong control and emphasis on discipline.

Democratic

Cooperation, shared responsibility and meaningful participation.

Liberal

Considerable learner freedom with limited teacher intervention.

05 / Psychological Foundations

Psychological Competence

Empathy

Understanding students' emotions and perspectives.

Active Listening

Giving thoughtful attention to students' concerns.

Emotional Intelligence

Recognizing and regulating emotions constructively.

Tolerance

Respecting individual differences and diverse experiences.

06 / Person-Centered Approach

Carl Rogers: Empathy and Acceptance

Carl Rogers (1902–1987)

American psychologist and influential theorist of humanistic psychology.

Freedom to Learn (1969)

Three Core Conditions

  • Empathy
  • Genuineness
  • Unconditional positive regard

Learning is supported by an atmosphere of acceptance and understanding.
